# ZIROC Website Design Direction

## Three Initial Approaches

### Theme Name: Clinical Precision
A restrained medical-technology direction using white space, deep navy, surgical green, and technical diagrams. It should feel credible to physiotherapists and investors without becoming sterile or hospital-generic.

**Probability:** 0.06

### Theme Name: Kerala Instrument
A warmer editorial direction that blends South Indian material cues, earthy neutrals, and documentary photography with precision engineering. It would position ZIROC as locally rooted and human-centered.

**Probability:** 0.03

### Theme Name: Signal / Motion
A dark, high-contrast robotics direction with cobalt light trails, control-system motifs, and cinematic product framing. It would feel more frontier-tech and investor-facing, but risks over-indexing on spectacle.

**Probability:** 0.08

## Chosen Direction: Clinical Precision

### Design Movement
Contemporary clinical-industrial design: the visual language of a high-trust medical device company combined with the quiet precision of an engineering lab. The page should feel like a credible bridge between a physiotherapy clinic, a robotics workbench, and an investor briefing.

### Core Principles
1. **Proof before theatre.** Every visual claim is labeled as concept, PoC, target, estimate, or validated evidence.
2. **Calm authority.** Use generous whitespace, clear hierarchy, and restrained accents instead of loud gradients or startup clichés.
3. **Human usefulness.** Keep the therapist and patient workflow visible, not only the machine.
4. **Engineering legibility.** Translate complex architecture into concise, understandable modules and measurable specifications.

### Color Philosophy
The primary canvas is warm clinical white rather than pure digital white. Deep ink navy communicates reliability and technical seriousness. A single ownable green, called **VALC Green**, marks movement, assistance, and verified action. Pale blue-grey surfaces separate sections like annotated sheets in a clinical engineering notebook. Green should never be used as decoration without meaning; it signals motion, feedback, progress, or a clear call to action.

### Layout Paradigm
Use an editorial, asymmetric rhythm instead of centered marketing blocks. The hero should split a confident narrative column from a large product visual. Subsequent sections should alternate between full-width statements, left-anchored technical explanations, and right-anchored evidence panels. Use measured horizontal rules, numbered sections, and generous negative space to create a document-like flow. On mobile, collapse into a clear single-column reading path without hiding the product story.

### Signature Elements
1. **Calibration line:** thin horizontal rules with small numbered labels that feel like engineering annotations.
2. **VALC Green signal:** short green bars, data highlights, and action labels used consistently for motion and progress.
3. **Spec strip:** compact technical callouts such as 48V system, 80Nm peak torque, and target setup under 2 minutes, always paired with confidence language where appropriate.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should feel like operating a well-designed clinical instrument: direct, predictable, and quiet. Navigation should scroll to clearly named sections. Buttons should use short action labels such as “Explore VALC” and “Talk to ZIROC.” Hover states should reveal structure through color and a short underline or lift, never gimmicky effects. All interactive elements must remain keyboard accessible and respect reduced-motion preferences.

### Animation
Use small, purposeful entrance motion only when it improves reading order: the hero text can rise gently, the product image can settle into place, and metric rows can reveal sequentially. Keep transitions between 160–260ms with an ease-out curve. Do not animate numerical claims, technical diagrams, or safety language in ways that imply real-time performance. Disable non-essential motion for users who prefer reduced motion.

### Typography System
Use **Space Grotesk** for display headings and **DM Sans** for body copy, labels, and technical metadata. Headlines should be compact, assertive, and sentence case. Body copy should remain readable at 16–18px with generous line height. Use tabular numerals for market and specification figures where possible. Avoid all-caps except for small section labels.

### Brand Essence
**ZIROC builds practical robotic rehabilitation systems for the clinics and therapists that imported platforms leave behind.**

Personality: **precise, grounded, determined.**

### Brand Voice
Headlines are direct and specific. CTAs are invitational, not inflated. Microcopy should distinguish what exists today from what is being built next.

Example headline: “Rehabilitation robotics, designed for the clinic floor.”

Example CTA: “See how VALC is engineered.”

### Wordmark & Logo
Use the supplied official ZIROC symbol as the primary mark. Keep it visually prominent in the header and use a small monochrome favicon derived from the supplied transparent logo where supported. Do not redraw the wordmark in a default font or replace the supplied identity with generated typography.

### Signature Brand Color
**VALC Green — #0B8F74.** It is an active, clinical green: calmer than neon, more ownable than generic teal, and reserved for assistance, feedback, progress, and high-intent actions.

## Implementation Reminder
Every page and component must reinforce the chosen direction. The site must clearly distinguish the current **VALC** development-stage system, its 1-DOF PoC, concept rendering, planning assumptions, and future validation work. Scrapped concept names must not appear anywhere.

## Style Decisions
- VALC is the sole current product name.
- Concept art must be labeled “Concept rendering — target V1 design.”
- Market figures from ZIROCmarket.pdf must be labeled as modeled, estimated, or planning assumptions where applicable.
- No purple gradients, generic SaaS dashboards, excessive rounded cards, or unsupported clinical efficacy claims.
